Species: Python regius (Ball Python) — Care Reference

TEMPERATURE & HUMIDITY
Ball pythons need a warm side of 88-92°F and a cool side of 78-80°F, with a
humidity range of 50-60% (raised to 60-70% briefly during shedding to support
a clean shed). Consistently low humidity is the most common cause of
incomplete or "stuck" sheds in captivity.

DIET
Strict carnivore, fed appropriately-sized rodents (mice or rats depending on
snake size) on a schedule of roughly every 1-2 weeks for adults, more
frequently for juveniles. It is completely normal for a ball python to
refuse food occasionally, especially during shedding, breeding season, or
after a stressful environment change — this alone is rarely cause for alarm
unless prolonged (multiple months) or paired with weight loss.

BEHAVIOUR & STRESS
Ball pythons are a naturally shy, low-activity species; balling up defensively
(the trait they're named for) is a normal stress response to feeling
threatened, not a sign of poor health. Frequent handling, incorrect
temperatures, and enclosure changes are the most common stressors and can
directly trigger feeding refusal.

COMMON HEALTH ISSUES
Respiratory infections (from incorrect temperature/humidity), mites, and
incomplete sheds (from low humidity) are the most frequently seen issues.
