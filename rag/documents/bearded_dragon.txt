Species: Pogona vitticeps (Bearded Dragon) — Care Reference

TEMPERATURE & HUMIDITY
Bearded dragons need a basking spot of 95-110°F with a cool side around
75-85°F, dropping to 65-75°F at night. Humidity should stay low, around
30-40% — this is an arid-adapted species, and excess humidity risks
respiratory infection. Full-spectrum UVB lighting is essential, not optional;
without it, calcium cannot be metabolised properly regardless of diet.

DIET
Omnivorous, with diet ratio shifting by age: juveniles need more insect
protein (crickets, dubia roaches, gut-loaded and dusted with calcium), adults
shift toward more leafy greens and vegetables as the larger diet component.
Avoid avocado, rhubarb, and insects sourced from pesticide-exposed areas.

BEHAVIOUR & STRESS
Arm-waving and head-bobbing are normal social/territorial displays, not
distress signals. Black "stress beard" darkening, glass-surfing (repeatedly
scratching at enclosure walls), and appetite loss are the clearer signs of a
genuine stressor — commonly incorrect temperatures, an overly small enclosure,
or a perceived threat from outside the enclosure (other pets, reflections).

COMMON HEALTH ISSUES
Metabolic bone disease (inadequate UVB/calcium) and impaction (from loose
substrate ingestion or low temperatures slowing digestion) are the most
frequent issues. Regular weighing is a strong early indicator of health changes.
