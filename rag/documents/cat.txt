Species: Felis catus (Domestic Cat) — Care Reference

ENVIRONMENT
Cats are comfortable across normal indoor room temperatures (68-78°F) and don't
require enclosure heating/humidity control the way reptiles do. They do
strongly prefer stable, predictable environments — sudden changes in household
routine, new pets, or rearranged furniture can raise baseline stress for days.

DIET
Cats are obligate carnivores and need a diet built around animal protein; they
cannot thrive on plant-based diets. Fresh water should always be available —
many cats naturally under-drink, so wet food helps meet hydration needs.
Toxic foods to strictly avoid: onion, garlic, chocolate, grapes/raisins, and
xylitol. Sudden diet changes should be introduced gradually over several days
to avoid digestive upset.

BEHAVIOUR & STRESS
Common stress indicators include hiding more than usual, reduced grooming (or
excessive over-grooming), decreased appetite, and increased vocalisation.
Loud appliances (vacuums), unfamiliar animals, and changes to feeding routine
are frequent stress triggers. Cats are highly routine-oriented — keeping
feeding and play times consistent measurably reduces stress-related behaviour.

COMMON HEALTH ISSUES
Dental disease, obesity, and urinary tract issues are among the most common
health problems in adult cats. Annual wellness exams and core vaccinations
(FVRCP, rabies where required) are standard preventive care. Sudden changes
in litter box habits often signal an underlying health issue worth a vet visit.
