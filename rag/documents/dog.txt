Species: Canis familiaris (Domestic Dog) — Care Reference

ENVIRONMENT
Dogs are comfortable in normal indoor/outdoor temperature ranges but breed
coat type matters — thick-coated breeds (like Golden Retrievers) are more
heat-sensitive than short-coated breeds and need shade and water access in
warm weather. Regular exercise appropriate to breed and age is essential for
both physical and behavioural health.

DIET
Puppies need large-breed-appropriate puppy formula to support controlled
growth (rapid growth in large breeds raises joint problems); adults transition
to maintenance food around 12-18 months depending on breed size. Toxic foods
to strictly avoid: chocolate, grapes/raisins, onion, garlic, xylitol, and
cooked bones (splinter risk). Feeding schedule consistency matters more than
exact timing.

BEHAVIOUR & STRESS
Separation anxiety (destructive behaviour, excessive vocalisation when left
alone), noise phobias (thunderstorms, fireworks), and under-socialisation are
the most common behavioural stressors, especially in young or newly-adopted
dogs. Consistent daily structure, adequate exercise, and gradual desensitisation
to known triggers meaningfully reduce anxiety-driven behaviour over time.

COMMON HEALTH ISSUES
Hip/joint issues (especially large breeds), dental disease, and obesity are
common. Puppies need a full vaccination series (DHPP, rabies) plus microchipping
before being considered fully protected; boosters continue on a regular
schedule into adulthood.
