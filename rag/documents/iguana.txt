Species: Iguana iguana (Green Iguana) — Care Reference

TEMPERATURE & HUMIDITY
Green iguanas need a strong thermal gradient: a basking spot of 95-100°F and a
cooler side of 80-85°F, with nighttime lows no colder than 75°F. Ambient humidity
should stay around 65-75%. Prolonged exposure below 80°F slows digestion badly and
can lead to impaction; this is the single most common husbandry mistake with
captive iguanas.

DIET
Iguanas are strict herbivores. Diet should be dark leafy greens (collard, mustard,
turnip greens, escarole) as the base, with other vegetables and occasional fruit
as a smaller portion. Never feed animal protein, dairy, or avocado. Calcium
supplementation (with D3 if UVB exposure is limited) 2-3 times per week is
essential to prevent metabolic bone disease.

BEHAVIOUR & STRESS
Iguanas are naturally shy and can be highly stressed by sudden handling, loud
noise, and unfamiliar people. Head-bobbing and dewlap extension are normal
territorial/communication displays, not necessarily aggression. Tail-whipping and
darkened colouration are more reliable stress signals. A consistent daily routine
significantly reduces baseline stress over time.

COMMON HEALTH ISSUES
Metabolic bone disease (from inadequate calcium/UVB), mouth rot, and impaction
from low enclosure temperatures are the most frequent issues seen in captive
iguanas. Regular weight tracking is one of the best early-warning signs of a
developing health problem.
