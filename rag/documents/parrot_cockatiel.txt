Species: Psittacidae (Parrots, incl. Cockatiels & Budgies) — Care Reference

ENVIRONMENT
Most companion parrot species are comfortable at normal indoor room
temperatures (65-80°F) and are sensitive to drafts, temperature swings, and
cooking fumes (non-stick cookware fumes are lethal to birds — a critical
household hazard). Consistent daytime light exposure (10-12 hours) with a
quiet, dark sleep period supports healthy behaviour.

DIET
A quality pelleted diet as the base, supplemented with fresh vegetables and
limited fruit, is healthier long-term than a seed-only diet, which tends to
be nutritionally deficient in fat-soluble vitamins. Toxic foods to avoid:
avocado, chocolate, caffeine, onion, garlic, and xylitol.

BEHAVIOUR & STRESS
Parrots are highly social and intelligent; prolonged isolation is itself a
significant stressor and can lead to feather-plucking or excessive screaming.
Common stress indicators: feather plucking, reduced vocalisation/activity,
fluffed-up posture held for extended periods, and appetite changes. Sudden
cage relocation, new people/pets, and schedule disruption are frequent
triggers — a predictable daily routine meaningfully reduces baseline stress.

COMMON HEALTH ISSUES
Feather-destructive behaviour (often stress or nutrition-linked), respiratory
issues from poor air quality, and obesity from seed-heavy diets are common.
Annual avian-vet wellness checks are recommended, as birds instinctively mask
illness until it's advanced.
