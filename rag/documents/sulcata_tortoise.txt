Species: Centrochelys sulcata (Sulcata / African Spurred Tortoise) — Care Reference

TEMPERATURE & HUMIDITY
Sulcatas need a basking area of 95-100°F with an ambient gradient down to 75-85°F,
and should never experience prolonged temperatures below 70°F. Despite coming from
arid regions, hatchlings and juveniles benefit from moderate humidity (50-70%) to
support healthy shell growth — low humidity is a leading cause of shell pyramiding
in captive-raised sulcatas. Adults tolerate drier conditions better than juveniles.

DIET
Sulcatas are grazing herbivores and need a high-fibre, low-protein, low-sugar diet:
primarily grasses and hay, with leafy greens as a supplement. Avoid fruit almost
entirely (it causes digestive upset and encourages parasitic overgrowth), and never
feed legume-heavy "tortoise mixes" long-term — excess protein accelerates shell
pyramiding and kidney issues.

BEHAVIOUR & STRESS
Sulcatas are generally calm but can show clear stress through reduced basking time,
pacing at enclosure edges, and reluctance to eat. Being lifted abruptly (rather than
supported securely from below) is a common stressor, as is cold drafts and damp,
unclean substrate. A settling-in period of 1-2 weeks after any environment change is
completely normal — reduced appetite during this window is not usually cause for
alarm unless it persists beyond that.

COMMON HEALTH ISSUES
Shell pyramiding (poor humidity/diet during growth), respiratory infections (from
cold/damp conditions), and metabolic bone disease are the most common issues. Sulcatas
grow very large (up to 150+ lbs) and need enclosure space planned years in advance.
